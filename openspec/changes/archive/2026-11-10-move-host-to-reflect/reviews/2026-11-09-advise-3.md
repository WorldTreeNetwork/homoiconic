## Conclusions from reading

The root cause is precise: parseArms installs guard := .col a at Read.lean:540. parsePattern consumes both brackets and everything between them through advance, without suspending that guard (:284–293). The guard is cleared only after the arrow (:543), so the delimiter exception never applies inside a pattern.

Outside that defect, guard lifetime looks sound: successful fn, let, def and arm headers clear it before parsing a body (:398,480,490,543). Their header grammars cannot recursively parse another expression before clearing it. The arm exemption points to the pattern's first token; advance consumes that position before any continuation, and the parser never rewinds. I found no exemption bypass admitting a later dedented header token and no guard left active after a successful header.

The task claims at tasks.md:14–15 accurately describe the added cases and completed catch-all matrix, but the design's bracket guarantee remains false until the blocking regression is fixed. The surface and zig-host deltas otherwise remain consistent with the reviewed code: names are resolved in Lean; runText uses runUntil budget (load (compile t)) only after successful reading (Run.lean:10–13); the numeric-code prohibition is scoped to reflect_run; legacy kernel_run checks remain explicitly documented. The living zig-host overview still needs its previously noted transition update when folding; that is non-blocking here.
