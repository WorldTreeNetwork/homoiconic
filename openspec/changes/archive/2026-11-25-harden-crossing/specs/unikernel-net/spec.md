# unikernel-net

## MODIFIED Requirements

### Requirement: Two guests run one program and record the crossing
<!-- base: c457c4d93aac7db10af8be910af02bec26be6e6e openspec/specs/unikernel-net/spec.md -->
The command line MAY carry `reflect.wire_port=<port>` or `reflect.peer=<a.b.c.d>:<port>` as before, and with either MAY carry `reflect.spawn=far|near`, `reflect.take=old|new`, `reflect.budget=<steps>` (1 to 2^64 − 1, the guest's own step limit), `reflect.turn=<cells>` (1 to 2^64 − 1, its own turn space) and `reflect.hold=<cells>` (a whole number below 2^64, its hold), each at most once; any of these without an end of the crossing SHALL be refused as other bad configuration is (`unikernel/net.zig` `parse`). Each guest SHALL begin a Lean guest with `reflect_cross_start`, passing its step limit, turn space and hold (0 for the defaults of `openspec/specs/crossing/spec.md`), and hold it between calls; Zig SHALL NOT read a packet, a batch, an entry or a process. The guest with `reflect.wire_port` is the one given the program, and it holds its connections in the table of the requirement "A serving guest admits connections into a bounded table": a stream on the wire port that starts `RFL1` SHALL be judged as a run frame is (kind 0, status 0, a budget in range), its body and budget SHALL be handed to `reflect_cross_give`, the budget a ceiling on the guest's own, and it SHALL be answered with a run reply frame with no body. Any other stream SHALL be a batch: the serving guest SHALL take batches from one entity only, the first that sent one, hand each to `reflect_cross_take`, call `reflect_cross_work` until Lean says nothing is possible (at most 64 calls), and answer with the bytes of `reflect_cross_batch`. The guest with `reflect.peer` SHALL open one QUIC connection per exchange from UDP port 9000, answering a Retry as the host client does, send `reflect_cross_batch`, hand the answer to `reflect_cross_take`, work, and open the next exchange no sooner than 20 ms after the last answer; an exchange with no answer in 8 seconds SHALL be abandoned and the batch sent again on a new connection. `reflect_cross_work` SHALL count the cells the Lean guest holds with `reflect_live_cells` when its ledger asks. When the Lean guest reports closed, each guest SHALL print its record on serial, from a buffer of 16 MiB taken from the heap when it prints (a static buffer of that size kept QUIC from answering), one `reflect: cross ` line for each line of `reflect_cross_record`, then `reflect: cross done`. Each guest has its entity key and `reflect.allow` as every QUIC guest does: the serving guest allows the other guest and the client that hands it the program, the calling guest allows the serving one. Before the record's lines a guest SHALL print `reflect: cross sig <public key> <signature>`, both hex: its Ed25519 signature, under its entity key, over the record's text as Lean gave it. The record's `me` SHALL be the text of the guest's own entity name and its `peer` the name of the entity its batches came from or went to (`Conn.peer_name` of the first such connection); a later connection from another allowed name SHALL carry no batch. A calling guest whose peer it does not allow SHALL print `reflect: the peer is not allowed`, and one whose handshake is given up `reflect: the peer gave no answer`, each once, and SHALL open the exchange again later. A guest whose Lean guest reports failure SHALL print its error line and power off.

#### Scenario: Two guests on one bridge

- GIVEN guest A booted with its key, `reflect.wire_port=4446 reflect.allow=<B's name>,<the client's name> reflect.spawn=far`, handed `examples/cross-race.hom` as bytes with budget 100000 by `quic-client`, and guest B booted with its key and `reflect.peer=<A>:4446 reflect.allow=<A's name>`
- WHEN B calls A
- THEN within 90 seconds both serial logs end with `reflect: cross done`, A's record has seven entries and B's six, they are the entries two guests in one Lean process record (`kernel/CrossCheck.lean`), and both records begin `form 2` and say `budget 100000 500000`

#### Scenario: A program that holds more and more on a guest

- GIVEN guest A handed `examples/cross-grow-forever.hom`, with the default hold, and separately with `reflect.hold=18446744073709551615`
- WHEN B calls A
- THEN with the default hold both guests close, A's record has a `full` entry and the check accepts it; with the hold at its largest A ends in `INTERNAL PANIC: out of memory`

### Requirement: The wire check replays the guests' records
<!-- base: c457c4d93aac7db10af8be910af02bec26be6e6e openspec/specs/unikernel-net/spec.md -->
`unikernel/check-wire.sh` SHALL require KVM and Cloud Hypervisor v54.0, build with `build-net.sh`, run `kernel/CrossingContract.lean` and refuse an axiom outside `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, run `kernel/CrossCheck.lean` and require the `ok` lines `host/check.sh` requires and no `FAIL`, and then, for each run, boot the serving guest, hand it the program with `quic-client`, boot the calling guest, wait for both to close, read both records off the serial logs, and run `unikernel/out-net/cross-check check` on the program's bytes and the two records. It SHALL require `accepted` and the expected process lines for `examples/cross-reply.hom`, the same program again while the host floods the serving guest's port from its own address with `quic-client ... flood` so that its first-flight pool is full and every exchange of the calling guest goes through a Retry (the run must close within 30 seconds, while the flood lasts, and the flooder must have heard Retries), `examples/cross-name.hom`, and `examples/cross-race.hom` under `reflect.take=old` and `reflect.take=new`; for the last two it SHALL require one program, two different records, `[1, 2]` and `[2, 1]` reported by the guest, and the two schedules, and SHALL require A's record of one with B's of the other refused with `the acknowledgements of b are not the order a took them in`. It SHALL run `examples/cross-reply.hom` with B given `reflect.budget=18446744073709551615 reflect.turn=18446744073709551615` and require both records to say `budget 100000 500000`, and with B given `reflect.budget=50000 reflect.turn=300000` and require both to say `budget 50000 300000`; with the default hold, `examples/cross-grow-forever.hom`, `examples/cross-fan.hom` under `reflect.spawn=far` with budget 10000000, and `examples/cross-chatter.hom`, and require each accepted with `full a`, `full b` and `full a: entry 60000` respectively and no panic on either serial; `examples/cross-worst.hom` with budget 10000000, and require it accepted, a count of at least 1400000 before its largest local start, and no panic; `examples/cross-grow-forever.hom` with `reflect.hold=18446744073709551615` on A and require A to end in `INTERNAL PANIC: out of memory`; and `examples/cross-deep.hom` with `reflect.turn=4000000 reflect.hold=16000000` on both. It SHALL make a key for each guest, for the client and for a stranger on every run, require each guest's `reflect: entity` line and each record's `me` and `peer` to be the names of those keys, and require that a calling guest that allows another name says `the peer is not allowed` and one the serving guest does not allow says `the peer gave no answer`, with no crossing line on either serial. `unikernel/out-net/cross-check check` SHALL refuse a record with no `sig` line (`it is not signed`), one whose signature does not verify (`the signature is not valid`), and one signed by a key whose entity name is not the record's `me` (`it is not signed by the entity it names`), before Lean sees it. From the records of the `old` run the check SHALL make tampered records and require each refused with its reason: with the signature left as it was, an entry moved, dropped from the middle, repeated, and the last dropped (the signature); a record with no signature and one signed by the stranger; signed again with the guest's own key (`cross-check sign`), an entry moved, dropped from the middle and repeated (the link) and the last entry dropped (the head); and, with the chain made again by `cross-check reseal` and signed, a taking repeated, a taking dropped and an acknowledgement dropped (the merge), a local event repeated (the replay), and the takings in the other order (the order of the acknowledgements), and A's record of the `old` run with B's of the `new` run (the same); with a budget above the record's own limit (`its budget is above its own limit`); and, from a run of `examples/cross-hidden.hom`, its two local deliveries swapped with the chain made again and signed (the comparison, by the root). A record made again unchanged and signed SHALL be accepted. It SHALL remove the TAPs and the bridge on exit. That each refusal is made by the check it is credited to is shown by `kernel/cross-controls.py`, which undoes one check at a time in a copy of the kernel and requires the test to fail (`unikernel/evidence/cross-controls.log`).

#### Scenario: The check passes

- GIVEN morphie with Cloud Hypervisor 54.0
- WHEN `sh unikernel/check-wire.sh` runs
- THEN it prints `wire guests ok` and exits 0 (`unikernel/evidence/check-wire.log`)

### Requirement: A run that passes its space is a reply, and the guest goes on
<!-- base: c457c4d93aac7db10af8be910af02bec26be6e6e openspec/specs/unikernel-net/spec.md -->

The network image SHALL pass a space to `reflect_reply_bytes` for every run frame, over UDP and over QUIC: the kernel's default, or the number of the command-line word `reflect.space=<cells>`, a whole number below 2^64; a word that is not one SHALL stop the guest before it is ready, as a malformed `reflect.port` does. The `RFL1` frame SHALL NOT change: a sender chooses the step budget, the guest's owner chooses the space. A run that passes its space, and an answer larger written out than its space, SHALL be answered with the reply `81 07` and transport status 0, as a run out of steps is answered with `81 01`, and the guest SHALL serve the next request. A guest of a crossing runs each turn with no count under the turn space the two guests agreed on (`reflect.turn`, the requirement "Two guests run one program and record the crossing"), whatever `reflect.space` says, so that the record it writes replays the same elsewhere.

#### Scenario: A recursion that waits over UDP

- GIVEN a ready guest with 512 MiB and the default space
- WHEN the client sends `def deep x = [x, ...deep x]` applied to 0, and then `def build acc = build [0, ...acc]` applied to `[]`, each with the budget 100000000, and then factorial
- THEN the first two replies are `81 07`, each equal to the Linux process's, and factorial is answered after them in the same boot

#### Scenario: The limit undone

- GIVEN a guest started with `reflect.space=18446744073709551615`
- WHEN the client sends `deep` with the budget 100000000
- THEN the guest ends in `INTERNAL PANIC: out of memory` and answers nothing more
