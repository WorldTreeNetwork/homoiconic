## Blind independent take

Written before opening the design, tasks, or spec delta, from the proposal's Why, the living language partition, and ARCHITECTURE.md.

1. Pin a named fragment with explicit syntax and membership, so the theorem's domain is decidable and cannot shrink silently.
2. Pin executable Lean definitions as the semantics that both compilation and proofs use.
3. Pin an interpreter that is actually a term of the object language, with a concrete encoding of its program input.
4. Pin the exact equality statement, including environments, result representation, errors, and any evaluation bounds.
5. Require coverage of every quote-free term in the named fragment, including malformed operations and open terms if its syntax admits them.
6. Refuse to substitute quotation round-trips or a Lean-level wrapper around eval for the metacircular theorem.
7. Refuse an interpreter primitive that delegates the entire input to the host evaluator and makes the equality vacuous.
8. Require a credible termination or bounded-execution story and an honest boundary excluding quoted re-entry from the theorem.
9. Keep Zig initialization, allocation, and the ABI in the host change; this contract should deliver the Lean artifact and proof.
10. Tradeoff: a small, terminating fragment can make full equality tractable, provided its restrictions preserve a meaningful interpreter term.

## Comparison and steelman

The author answers much of the take. `Pure` explicitly covers all quote-free terms, de Bruijn binders and call-by-value order are named, the interpreter is a closed object-language term, and its program and outcome encodings are described. The quote-payload route avoids applying `Direct` to a quote. There is no evaluator primitive hidden inside the interpreter. The theorem has an explicit type, determinism is required, and axiom inspection excludes `sorryAx` and package-declared axioms.

Against my tenth concern, restricting the fragment to terminating terms is unnecessary: an inductive evaluation relation can express convergence without a fuel parameter, and the all-pure quantifier is a reasonable stronger contract. Scott-encoded outcomes are also a defensible choice; representation correspondence can express metacircular equality without literal equality of the two runtime values. Neither choice is a reason to reject this design. The remaining issues are inconsistencies and missing proof obligations within those choices.

## Findings that must be reconciled

### R3 — Acceptance examples require the wrong interpreter results

`design.md:42` defines `Represents` by decoding an object-level closure. Lines 44–45 encode success and failure as lambda terms. Consequently, the interpreter's successful return of an encoded `unbound 0` is a **closure representing that error**, not the raw `unbound 0` outcome of `Direct`. Likewise its encoded successful identity closure is wrapped in the outcome encoding; it is not literally `closure (var 0) []`.

Nevertheless, `specs/lean-kernel/spec.md:37` requires each raw outcome to be the original identity closure, and line 43 requires both routes to derive raw `unbound 0`. `design.md:69` and line 71 repeat those requirements. The strongest charitable reading is that “both routes” means the interpreter result *after decoding*. That is compatible with the theorem, but the actual scenarios say the routes derive those outcomes and omit the decoding step.

Reconcile the examples with the theorem: the direct route derives `o`; the interpreter route derives `w`; `Represents o w` holds. Make the two sample declarations check these distinct obligations. Do not satisfy the open-term example by making the interpreter itself fail with a raw unbound index.

### R4 — Application error propagation and the open-term claim disagree

The rule inventory at `design.md:10`–14 propagates an unbound **argument**, but gives no application rule for an unbound **function**. For example, `app (var 0) (lam (var 0))` has no derivation under those listed rules, despite function-first evaluation encountering `unbound 0` immediately. Line 15 then classifies that missing derivation as divergence. The interpreter application continuation at line 34 also assumes the function outcome is a closure without specifying its error branch.

The blanket sentence “Open terms derive `unbound`” at line 55 is independently false for this call-by-value semantics: `lam (var 1)` is open but returns a closure without evaluating its body. I can infer the intended behavior is that an *evaluated* unbound variable propagates, but that is a semantic rule the contract needs to state.

Pin function-error propagation on both routes and narrow the open-term sentence. Require a function-position error example whose argument diverges, such as `app (var 0) omega`, to establish that function-first error propagation skips the argument. An open lambda example should preserve the specified closure behavior.

### R5 — The theorem excludes represented results, not all spurious termination

The equivalence at `design.md:49`–52 says that a source outcome exists exactly when an interpreter outcome **representing it** exists. For a diverging source program, this excludes represented interpreter outcomes. It does not exclude the interpreter terminating with a closure that represents no source outcome. Determinism does not close that gap: a unique undecodable result still satisfies every instance of the displayed equivalence for that diverging program.

This matters because `design.md:55` and `specs/lean-kernel/spec.md:49` promise no derivation on the interpreter route, whereas the build obligation at `design.md:70` only promises no `Represents` witness. The concrete interpreter may well preserve the intended outcome encoding, but that preservation is an additional obligation, not a consequence of the displayed theorem.

Require a proved result-coverage property for interpreter executions on all pure inputs (every derived interpreter outcome represents some source outcome), or strengthen the correctness statement equivalently. Keep the omega check as absence of **any** interpreter derivation. This closes the gap without changing the chosen representation or adding fuel to metacircular equality.

### R6 — Executability is not tied to the proved semantics

The living partition requires a theorem about the evaluator source. Here `Direct` is an inductive relation, `Eval` extends that relation, and an executable witness is only optional (`design.md:15`). The build scenario at `specs/lean-kernel/spec.md:5`–11 requires a successful package build; it does not require a callable evaluation function or a connection between its computed results and `Direct`/`Eval`. A package of syntax and proofs could pass those checks without delivering the executable evaluator promised by the Why and the requirement title.

The charitable reading is that “executable kernel” already implies a function will be supplied. Make that deliverable and its correctness connection explicit. Require an executable Lean evaluation entry, its handling of nontermination or bounded execution, and proofs connecting its results to the relation used by `metacircular`; run the nontrivial application and open-variable examples through it. A bounded witness can retain a fuel-free relational theorem and need not use equal fuel for both routes. This asks for no Zig host, ABI, initialization, or allocation work.
