## Blind take

Written before opening design.md, tasks.md, or the proposed spec delta; based on the proposal's Why, the living language partition, ARCHITECTURE.md, and the packet's fragment boundary.

1. Pin one executable Lean 4 evaluator as both the runtime semantics and the object of the proof.
2. Pin the pure fragment by an explicit recursive predicate excluding quote throughout each term.
3. Pin the interpreter as a concrete term of that same language, not a host helper renamed as an interpreter.
4. Pin the representation of program syntax and the representation of interpreter inputs and outputs.
5. Pin the exact equality, its quantifiers, environments, and any termination or resource assumptions.
6. Require a fragment expressive enough to exercise genuine interpretation, with examples that distinguish interpretation from identity.

## Comparison and findings

The change answers the ownership and scope concerns: executable Lean owns evaluation, the theorem is a Lean declaration, pure programs exclude quote, and the host remains downstream. A small fragment and one interpretation level are appropriate. Two semantic contracts remain unresolved; neither requires broadening this change.

### R1 — Blocking: specify an interpreter term and its execution route

At `design.md:10–12`, direct evaluation takes pure terms, but the interpreter-term is a quoted representation of that Lean function. At `specs/lean-kernel/spec.md:15–21`, quote wraps an object-language term and its construction does not evaluate it. These statements do not supply an object-language encoding of the Lean function, an interpreter body, or the evaluation rule through which the quoted interpreter can run. The four constructors alone do not specify such a bridge.

The problem is not that an interpreter containing quote would automatically violate the theorem: the exclusion applies to input programs. The missing contract is how the left-hand side runs through the same semantics when its interpreter is not in the stated domain of direct evaluation. Specify the concrete interpreter term or its construction, the program-as-data encoding, and the evaluation/application route. Distinguish any permitted quote handling from quoted re-entry on input programs. The route must demonstrate object-language interpretation; a host branch that simply invokes direct evaluation on the payload would not establish the living partition's claim that the interpreter is written in the language.

### R2 — Blocking: state a meaningful equality over the promised fragment

At `specs/lean-kernel/spec.md:25–31`, the theorem ranges over every quote-free program. At `design.md:9–10`, values are closures and evaluation is a function, but evaluation order, environments, failure, divergence, and the equality's result representation are unspecified. The promised syntax includes the closed quote-free term `(λx. x x) (λx. x x)`. Under ordinary beta evaluation it reduces to itself, so postponing a recursion construct (`design.md:16`) does not make all promised programs terminate. Quote-free open variables also require an environment or an explicit unbound-variable outcome.

Write the proposed Lean theorem signature together with the evaluator's domain and codomain. State how nontermination and open terms are represented and how the interpreter's output is compared with a direct closure, including any encoding/decoding or observational relation. If evaluation uses fuel, account for interpretation overhead rather than assuming identical fuel gives equal results. A result about terminating runs can be appropriate, but its quantifiers and coverage must be reconciled explicitly with the current universal requirement; silently restricting purity to closed, typed, or normalizing terms would change this contract. The reader is not prescribing a particular partiality model.

## Steelman against the blind take

The strongest reading is that “quoted representation” is shorthand for a genuine object-language interpreter, and “function on pure terms” describes only the direct-program interface, not every internal evaluation operation. A broader Lean evaluator could then run the interpreter while the theorem still quantifies solely over quote-free inputs. Likewise, a small untyped lambda language can express substantial interpretation machinery without a primitive recursion constructor. These points defeat an objection that the architecture is inherently impossible, and they do not justify demanding more language features or a reflective tower.

They do not answer the two findings: the current documents leave those constructions and the exact proposition to the implementer. Different answers would change what circularity means and which outcomes the theorem equates. A short explicit term/encoding contract and theorem signature are sufficient preparation; a completed implementation or proof is not required for the next review.

## Verification owed with the amended contract

The build scenario (`specs/lean-kernel/spec.md:7–11`) and the three original tasks give no concrete evidence check beyond successful build and the requested theorem. Add verification for the chosen proposition: inspect the theorem's actual type and axiom dependencies, reject `sorry`/`admit` and newly assumed semantic/correctness axioms in its dependency path, and exercise a nontrivial closed pure application through both evaluation routes. Include the selected divergence/open-term behavior and a check that an input containing quote cannot satisfy the pure-fragment premise. Examples support the general proof; they do not replace it. This is verification of the existing instrument-level acceptance, not a request for a larger language.
