The following independent take was written before opening `design.md`, `tasks.md`, or the spec delta. Its inputs were only the proposal's Why, the living `language-partition` spec, and the cited architecture context. There is no living `lean-kernel` spec or product kernel to review.

1. Pin one named fragment with explicit syntax, values, environments, and evaluation behavior.
2. Pin the executable Lean evaluator as the exact function the theorem concerns.
3. Pin an interpreter expressed as an ordinary term of that fragment.
4. Pin the representation that lets this interpreter receive a program as data.
5. Require metacircular equality for every quote-free term, including open or failing terms as applicable.
6. State how termination, divergence, or fuel affects both sides of the equality.
7. Refuse a theorem that merely compares two aliases of the evaluator.
8. Refuse a hidden semantic primitive that performs the interpreter's work.
9. Keep quoted re-entry outside the theorem and Zig hosting outside this change.
10. Accept a deliberately small fragment in exchange for a precise, executable, non-vacuous proof.

Comparison with the contract:

- **Domain and evaluation:** `design.md:5–16` fixes de Bruijn terms, function-first call-by-value, closures, and unbound-index propagation. `Pure` excludes quotes everywhere without excluding open or diverging programs. The open lambda and function-error-before-diverging-argument scenarios distinguish errors, values, and divergence correctly (`specs/lean-kernel/spec.md:47–69`).
- **Actual object-language interpretation:** `design.md:20–38` supplies program encoding, encoded environments, and the three interpreter branches. The body must be a closed pure term built solely from the language constructors. Projecting it from its quote is syntactic access; evaluation then proceeds through `Direct` on the interpreter application. No evaluator-invoking primitive is permitted. This meets the partition's metacircular obligation without bringing quoted re-entry into the theorem.
- **Result correspondence:** `design.md:42–52` ties `Represents` to decoding the specified outcome encoding, rather than allowing an arbitrary relation. The application and open-variable checks now distinguish the source outcome `o` from the encoded closure `w`. Literal equality of these raw outcomes would be the wrong requirement.
- **Divergence:** The separate `coverage` declaration closes the gap left by represented-result equivalence alone (`design.md:57–64`, delta lines 31–33). If an interpreter derivation existed, coverage would supply an `o`; the reverse implication of `metacircular` would then supply a source derivation. Thus absence of all source derivations excludes every interpreter derivation, including malformed or unrepresented results. The omega check now demands that stronger property.
- **Executable semantics:** `design.md:74–85` requires total `evalFuel`, soundness and completeness against `Direct`, and declarations for those facts. The delta requires execution of both routes for the identity application and open variable. Separate fuel budgets permit interpreter overhead while preserving the same outcome correspondence. A finite `none` is not a certificate of divergence; the universal omega property is a proof obligation.
- **Verification and scope:** The contract requires Lean declarations for `metacircular` and `coverage`, excludes `sorryAx` and package-declared axioms from their dependencies, and requires proved determinism. The three implementation tasks remain open. Zig initialization, allocation, and ABI work stay with `add-zig-host`.

Steelman against the independent take: my second concern could be read as requiring the theorem to mention the executable function directly. The relation-based theorem plus proved soundness and completeness of the executable function answers the underlying concern without imposing a shared fuel budget or excluding divergence. Likewise, the quoted interpreter wrapper is acceptable because its closed pure payload performs the interpretation; quote itself supplies no semantic shortcut. Encoded outcomes and explicit decoding preserve exact source-result correspondence without requiring identical runtime closures.

The remaining construction choices—capture-safe de Bruijn assembly, call-by-value recursion and branch suspension, and a concrete decoder for represented closures—belong to implementation. They must satisfy the fixed signatures, encodings, and execution checks; they do not justify narrowing `Pure`, replacing decoding with observational equivalence, or introducing a host evaluator. This acceptance does not establish that those proofs have already been built.
